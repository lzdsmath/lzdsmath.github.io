Welcome to my website! I am a mathematician working at the interface between analysis, probability, and geometry, and a tenure-track Assistant Professor at the Università degli Studi di Roma "Tor Vergata".

Until October 2024, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Institute of Science and Technology Austria (ISTA) in the group of Jan Maas, and the principal investigator of the project Configuration Spaces over non-smooth Spaces funded by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) under the Early-Stage-Programme: Research–Innovation–Training (ESPRIT).

Previously, I obtained my doctoral degree at IAM Bonn, in the group of Karl-Theodor Sturm. I completed my bachelor and master studies in mathematics at Sapienza University in Rome, under the supervision of Adriano Pisante.

At ISTA I have been a member of the Sfb 65 Taming Complexity in Partial Differential Systems and at IAM of the Sfb 1060 The Mathematics of Emergent Effects in project B03 Optimal transport and random measures. I am currently an adjunct member of the DFG Priority Programme SPP 2265 in project 15 Random Riemannian Geometry.

    @misc{DelloSchiavoQuattrocchi2023Polychromatic,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo and Quattrocchi, Filippo},
  title         = {Polychromatic Partitions, {K}ingman Theory, and {E}wens Sampling Formula},
  year          = {2023},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2309.11292},
  eprint        = {2309.11292},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    A polychromatic partition is a partition of a set of colored (marked) elements in which each block is recorded only by the number of elements of each color in that block. We introduce a notion of polychromatic partition structure, i.e. a family of random polychromatic partitions consistent under deletion of elements uniformly at random, simultaneously extending (standard) partition structures, multipartition structures, and partition structures of partially exchangeable type. As an example thereof, we define a polychromatic analogue of the celebrated Ewens Sampling Formula, proving polychromatic versions of Kingman's characterization and of Hoppe's urn model. As our main result, we give a complete characterization of polychromatic partition structures, proving a triple affine homeomorphism of Bauer simplices among: polychromatic partition structures, harmonic densities for the polychromatic Hoppe urn, and probability measures on a polychromatic Kingman simplex. Additionally, we prove that this new simplex is homeomorphic to the Martin boundary of the polychromatic Hoppe urn inside its Martin compactification. We discuss various applications including closed non-recursive non-iterative expressions for multivariate moments of several random measures in terms of cycle index polynomials whose monomials are indexed by polychromatic partitions.

    @misc{DelloSchiavoPinamonti2026Global,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo and Pinamonti, Andrea},
  title         = {A `global' Perspective on the Differential Geometry of {W}asserstein Spaces},
  year          = {2026},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2607.27121},
  eprint        = {2607.27121},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    We develop a global differential calculus on the $L^2$-Wasserstein space over a closed Riemannian manifold, based on derivations of cylinder functions rather than on the standard pointwise approach. Within this framework we define some fundamental geometric tools. In particular, we show that the Levi-Civita connection on the base manifold lifts to the unique torsion-free connection compatible with the 'extended' Otto metric. The corresponding Riemann tensor is exactly the lift of the base Riemann tensor, which shows that — in this framework — the correction terms of the classical gradient formalism are not intrinsic curvature terms, but arise from the projection onto the measure-dependent gradient distribution. This allows us to revisit with a global and purely differential approach the smooth computations by J. Lott, Comm. Math. Phys., 277(2):423-437, 2007, reaching partially different conclusions.

    @misc{BrigatiDelloSchiavo2025Nonbilinear,
  author        = {Brigati, Giovanni and Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo},
  title         = {Non-bilinear {D}irichlet Functionals: {M}arkovianity, locality, invariance},
  year          = {2025},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2510.02942},
  eprint        = {2510.02942},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    We present in a unified setting the foundations for a theory of non-bilinear Dirichlet functionals on Hilbert spaces. We prove known and new equivalences between non-linear semigroups, non-linear resolvents, non-linear generators, and their energy functionals, including a complete characterization of Markovianity. We introduce and characterize strong notions of invariance for general lower semicontinuous convex functionals, and notions of locality and strong locality for non-bilinear Dirichlet functionals. Contrary to many partial results in the literature, these characterizations are complete and correctly extend the analogous assertions for the bilinear case in full generality.

    @misc{DelloSchiavoSodini2025HellingerKantorovich,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo and Sodini, Giacomo Enrico},
  title         = {The {H}ellinger--{K}antorovich metric measure geometry on spaces of measures},
  year          = {2025},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2503.07802},
  eprint        = {2503.07802},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    Let $(M,g)$ be a Riemannian manifold with Riemannian distance $\mathsf{d}_g$, and $\mathcal{M}(M)$ be the space of all non-negative Borel measures on $M$, endowed with the Hellinger-Kantorovich distance $\mathsf{H\! K}_{\mathsf{d}_g}$ induced by $\mathsf{d}_g$. Firstly, we prove that $(\mathcal{M}(M),\mathsf{H\! K}_{\mathsf{d}_g})$ is a universally infinitesimally Hilbertian metric space, and that a natural class of cylinder functions is dense in energy in the Sobolev space of every finite Borel measure on $\mathcal{M}(M)$. Secondly, we endow $\mathcal{M}(M)$ with its canonical reference measure, namely A.M. Vershik's multiplicative infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure $\mathcal{L}_\theta$, $\theta>0$, and we consider: (a) the geometric structure on $\mathcal{M}(M)$ induced by the natural action on $\mathcal{M}(M)$ of the semi-direct product of diffeomorphisms and densities on $M$, under which $\mathcal{L}_\theta$ is the unique invariant measure; and (b) the metric measure structure of $(\mathcal{M}(M),\mathsf{H\! K}_{\mathsf{d}_g},\mathcal{L}_{\theta})$, inherited from that of $(M,\mathsf{d}_g,\mathrm{vol}_g)$. We identify the canonical Dirichlet form $(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{E}))$ of (a) with the Cheeger energy of (b), thus proving that these two structures coincide. We further prove that $(\mathcal{E},\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{E}))$ is a conservative quasi-regular strongly local Dirichlet form on $\mathcal{M}(M)$, recurrent if and only if $\theta\in (0,1]$, and properly associated with the Brownian motion of the Hellinger-Kantorovich geometry on $\mathcal{M}(M)$.

    @misc{DelloSchiavo2024Massive,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo},
  title         = {Massive Particle Systems, {W}asserstein {B}rownian Motions, and the {D}ean--{K}awasaki Equation},
  year          = {2024},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2411.14936},
  eprint        = {2411.14936},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    We develop a unifying theory for four different objects:

    1. infinite systems of interacting massive particles;
    2. solutions to the Dean—Kawasaki equation with singular drift and space-time white noise;
    3. Wasserstein diffusions with a.s. purely atomic reversible random measures;
    4. metric measure Brownian motions induced by Cheeger energies on $L^2$-Wasserstein spaces.

    For the objects in (1)-(3) we prove existence and uniqueness of solutions, and several characterizations, on an arbitrary locally compact Polish ambient space $M$ with exponentially recurrent Feller driving noise. In the case of the Dean—Kawasaki equation, this amounts to replacing the Laplace operator with some arbitrary diffusive Markov generator $\mathsf{L}$ with ultracontractive semigroup. In addition to a complete discussion of the free case, we consider singular interactions, including, e.g., mean-field repulsive isotropic pairwise interactions of Riesz and logarithmic type under the assumption of local integrability. We further show that each Markov diffusion generator $\mathsf{L}$ on $M$ induces in a natural way a geometry on the space of probability measures over $M$. When $M$ is a manifold and $\mathsf{L}$ is a drifted Laplace-Beltrami operator, this geometry coincides with the geometry of $L^2$-optimal transportation. The corresponding 'geometric Brownian motion' coincides with the 'metric measure Brownian motion' in (4).

    @misc{DelloSchiavoSuzuki2022Configuration,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo and Suzuki, Kohei},
  title         = {Configuration spaces over singular spaces --- {II}. {C}urvature},
  year          = {2022},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2205.01379},
  eprint        = {2205.01379},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    This is the second paper of a series on configuration spaces $\Upsilon$ over singular spaces $X$. Here, we focus on geometric aspects of the extended metric measure space $(\Upsilon, \mathsf{d}_{\Upsilon}, \mu)$ equipped with the $L^2$-transportation distance $\mathsf{d}_{\Upsilon}$, and a mixed Poisson measure $\mu$. Firstly, we establish the essential self-adjointness and the $L^p$-uniqueness for the Laplacian on $\Upsilon$ lifted from $X$. Secondly, we prove the equivalence of Bakry–Émery curvature bounds on $X$ and on $\Upsilon$, without any metric assumption on $X$. We further prove the Evolution Variation Inequality on $\Upsilon$, and introduce the notion of synthetic Ricci-curvature lower bounds for the extended metric measure space $\Upsilon$. As an application, we prove the Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property on $\Upsilon$ over singular spaces $X$, originally conjectured in the case when $X$ is a manifold by M. Röckner and A. Schied. As a further application, we prove the $L^\infty$-to-$\mathsf{d}_{\Upsilon}$-Lipschitz regularization of the heat semigroup on $\Upsilon$ and give a new characterization of the ergodicity of the corresponding particle systems in terms of optimal transport.

    @misc{DelloSchiavoSuzuki2021Configuration,
  author        = {Dello Schiavo, Lorenzo and Suzuki, Kohei},
  title         = {Configuration spaces over singular spaces --- {I}. {D}irichlet-Form and Metric Measure Geometry},
  year          = {2021},
  note          = {Preprint, arXiv:2109.03192},
  eprint        = {2109.03192},
  archivePrefix = {arXiv}
}

    This paper is the first in a series on configuration spaces over singular spaces. Here, we construct a canonical differential structure on the configuration space $\Upsilon$ over a singular base space $X$ and with a general invariant measure $\mu$ on $\Upsilon$.
    We first present an analytic structure on $\Upsilon$, constructing a strongly local Dirichlet form $\mathcal E$ on $L^2(\Upsilon, \mu)$ for $\mu$ in a large class of probability measures. We then investigate the geometric structure of the extended metric measure space $\Upsilon$ endowed with the $L^2$-transportation extended distance $\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon$ and with the measure $\mu$. By establishing various Rademacher- and Sobolev-to-Lipschitz-type properties for $\mathcal E$, we finally provide a complete identification of the analytic and the geometric structure — the canonical differential structure induced on $\Upsilon$ by $X$ and $\mu$ — showing that $\mathcal E$ coincides with the Cheeger energy of $(\Upsilon,\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon,\mu)$ and that the intrinsic distance of $\mathcal E$ coincides with $\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon$.
    The class of base spaces to which our results apply includes sub-Riemannian manifolds, $\mathsf{RCD}$ spaces, locally doubling metric measure spaces satisfying a local Poincaré inequality, and path/loop spaces over Riemannian manifolds; as for $\mu$, our results include Campbell measures and quasi-Gibbs measures, in particular: Poisson measures, canonical Gibbs measures, as well as some determinantal/permanental point processes (sine$_\beta$, Airy$_\beta$, Bessel$_{\alpha, \beta}$, Ginibre).
    A number of applications to interacting particle systems and infinite-dimensional metric measure geometry are also discussed. In particular, we prove the quasi-regularity of $\mathcal E$ and consequently the existence of a Markov diffusion associated to $\mathcal E$ describing a $\mu$-invariant particle system. We further show the universality of the $L^2$-transportation distance $\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon$ for the Varadhan short-time asymptotics for diffusions on $\Upsilon$, regardless of the choice of $\mu$. Our approach does not rely on any relation between $\mu$ and a possible smooth structure on $X$. In particular, we assume no quasi-invariance property of $\mu$ w.r.t. actions of any group of transformations of $X$. Many of our results are new even in the case of standard Euclidean spaces.

Wasserstein Diffusions, Particle Systems, and the Dean–Kawasaki Equation


a $\mathcal{D}$-typical measure on the square

My PhD thesis constructs the Dirichlet–Ferguson diffusion, a canonical diffusion on the $L^2$-Wasserstein space $\mathscr{P}_2(M)$ over a closed Riemannian manifold $M$ of dimension $d\geq 2$, reversible with respect to the Dirichlet–Ferguson measure. It is the multi-dimensional counterpart of Konarovskyi's Modified Massive Arratia Flow.

More recently, I developed a unifying theory for four objects: infinite systems of interacting massive particles, solutions to the Dean–Kawasaki equation with singular drift, Wasserstein diffusions with purely atomic reversible random measures, and metric measure Brownian motions on $L^2$-Wasserstein spaces. The theory works on general locally compact Polish spaces, with the Laplacian replaced by an arbitrary diffusive Markov generator $\mathsf{L}$, and includes singular mean-field interactions of Riesz and logarithmic type. Each such $\mathsf{L}$ induces a geometry on the space of probability measures, which reduces to $L^2$-optimal transport when $\mathsf{L}$ is a drifted Laplace–Beltrami operator. With V. Konarovskyi, I showed that the pure-noise Dean–Kawasaki equation is ill-posed: it has no solutions for any initial measure and any bounded drift, a sharp result.

I am also interested in scaling limits of interacting particle systems. With L. Portinale and F. Sau, I derived the hydrodynamic and hydrostatic limits of the open symmetric exclusion and inclusion processes on Lipschitz domains, with Neumann, Robin, or Dirichlet boundary conditions depending on the strength of the boundary reservoirs.

Geometry of Spaces of Measures

I study the differential and metric measure structure of spaces of measures, starting from a Rademacher-type theorem on $\mathscr{P}_2(M)$. With G.E. Sodini, I showed that the space $\mathcal{M}(M)$ of all non-negative measures on a Riemannian manifold, endowed with the Hellinger–Kantorovich distance, is universally infinitesimally Hilbertian, and that its canonical Dirichlet form with respect to Vershik's multiplicative infinite-dimensional Lebesgue measure $\mathcal{L}_\theta$ coincides with the Cheeger energy. The associated Brownian motion is recurrent if and only if $\theta\in(0,1]$. With A. Pinamonti, I am developing a global differential calculus on the Wasserstein space, based on derivations of cylinder functions. In this framework the Levi-Civita connection and the Riemann tensor of the base manifold lift to the Wasserstein space, and the correction terms of the classical gradient formalism come from the projection onto the gradient distribution rather than from intrinsic curvature.

Configuration Spaces

Together with Kohei Suzuki, I am developing a series of works on the configuration space $\Upsilon$ over a metric measure space $X$. Here $\Upsilon$ is endowed with the $L^2$-transportation distance $\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon$ and with a reference measure $\mu$, such as a Poisson, Gibbs, or quasi-Gibbs measure (e.g. sine$_\beta$, Airy$_\beta$, Bessel, Ginibre). In the first part we construct the canonical differential structure on $\Upsilon$, showing that the Dirichlet form lifted from $X$ coincides with the Cheeger energy of $(\Upsilon,\mathsf{d}_\Upsilon,\mu)$, with no smoothness of $X$ and no quasi-invariance of $\mu$ required. In the second part we transfer Bakry–Émery curvature bounds from $X$ to $\Upsilon$ and prove the Sobolev-to-Lipschitz property conjectured by M. Röckner and A. Schied. With R. Herry and K. Suzuki, I constructed an optimal-transport geometry on the Poisson space, for which the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck semigroup is the gradient flow of the relative entropy and the Ricci curvature is bounded below by $1$.

Dirichlet Forms, Linear and Non-linear

Much of the above rests on results for Dirichlet spaces in full generality. With K. Suzuki, I studied the Rademacher and Sobolev-to-Lipschitz properties of intrinsic distances, their persistence under localization, tensorization, and direct integration, and the resulting Varadhan short-time asymptotics. I also constructed ergodic decompositions of quasi-regular Dirichlet spaces, and studied their behavior under order isomorphisms with M. Wirth.

A newer direction is non-linear. With G. Brigati, I laid the foundations of a theory of non-bilinear Dirichlet functionals on Hilbert spaces, with complete characterizations of Markovianity, invariance, and (strong) locality. In October 2026 I co-organized, with M. Braun, G. Brigati, and M. Rehmeier, the workshop Nonlinear Markov Processes, Dirichlet Forms, and PDEs at the Bernoulli Center, EPFL.

Gradient Flows and Synthetic Curvature

I am interested in gradient flows of functionals on metric spaces. With J. Maas and F. Pedrotti, I gave local conditions of Kurdyka–Łojasiewicz type, stated only in terms of the initial datum, for the global convergence of gradient flows and proximal point sequences, with sharp rates. With M. Magnabosco and C. Rigoni, I studied $(K,N)$-convex functionals with negative $N$, which may take both values $\pm\infty$, proving contractivity, regularity, and uniqueness of their gradient flows in the sense of Evolution Variational Inequalities.

Random Riemannian Geometry

As an adjunct member of project 15 Random Riemannian Geometry of the DFG Priority Programme SPP 2265, I work with R. Herry, E. Kopfer, and K.-T. Sturm on random geometries over manifolds of arbitrary even dimension $n$. Replacing the Laplace–Beltrami operator with its conformally covariant counterpart, the GJMS operator of critical order $n$, we construct conformally invariant log-correlated Gaussian fields, the associated quantum Liouville measures and Liouville Brownian motion, and a Polyakov–Liouville measure with its conformal anomaly. On tori of arbitrary dimension we proved the discrete-to-continuum convergence of polyharmonic fields and of their Liouville Quantum Gravity measures for all $|\gamma|<\sqrt{2n}$. In the regular regime, Fractional Gaussian Fields give random conformal perturbations of a Riemannian metric, and we study how diameter, volume, heat kernel, and spectral gap respond to the noise.

Random Measures and Exchangeable Partitions

The Dirichlet–Ferguson measure, the invariant measure of the diffusion above, is a central object in Bayesian non-parametrics and in the theory of random measures; I computed its characteristic functional over locally compact Polish spaces. With F. Quattrocchi, I introduced polychromatic partitions, partitions of colored elements in which each block records only how many elements of each color it contains. We define a polychromatic Ewens Sampling Formula and Hoppe urn, and prove a complete Kingman-type characterization of polychromatic partition structures, with applications to the moments of random measures.

  1. WDP

    This Demonstration shows the evolution of a partially asymmetric simple exclusion process (PASEP) on the integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}.$ In PASEP, a particle waits an exponential amount of time, then moves to the right with probability $p$ or to the left with probability $1-p$, unless the site is occupied. In the case $p=1$, the dynamic is totally asymmetric, and the process is known as a totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP).

  3. WDP

    Given $X=\{a_1,\dotsc, a_r\}$ a set of generators for $\mathbb{R}^n$, the zonotope $\mathcal{Z}(X)$ generated by $X$ is the convex hull of all vectors of the form $\sum_{a\in S} a$ with $S\subseteq X$; that is, $\mathcal{Z}(X)$ is the Minkowski sum of all segments $[0,a]$, where $a\in X$. Also, $\mathcal{Z}(X)$ is the shadow of the $r$-dimensional cube $[0,1]^r$ via the projection $(t_1,\dotsc, t_r)\mapsto \sum_i^r t_i a_i$.
